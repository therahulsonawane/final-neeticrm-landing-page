**The problem:** your telecallers report plenty of site visits scheduled, but the site team sees half of them turn up. Leads look healthy on paper and bookings stay low.

The site visit is the hinge of the funnel. Before it, you are an advert and a voice. After it, you are a place the buyer has seen. There are two separate problems: getting qualified buyers to say yes, and getting them to arrive.

## Part 1: Getting the yes

**Qualify first.** Inviting every lead wastes your site team's time. Find out budget, unit type, timeline, who decides and what they have already seen. If the project does not fit, say so; trust today means referrals tomorrow.

**Offer a specific visit.** "Visit sometime" fails. "Saturday at 11 or Sunday at 4?" works, because choosing is easier than deciding.

**Sell the experience.** Tell them what they will see: the sample flat, the floor they are considering, the amenities.

**Handle "let me think".** Ask what they want to think about, and give a reason to move, such as an available unit or a family visit. See our [objection handling guide](/blog/real-estate-objection-handling/).

## Part 2: Making sure they show up

Track scheduled and completed visits separately. The gap is a leak you can usually fix cheaply.

1. **Confirm immediately** on WhatsApp with date, time, address and a location pin.
2. **Remind twice**, the day before and the morning of, with a site contact and an easy way to reschedule.
3. **Remove friction** with directions, parking details or pickup help.
4. **Brief the site team** on who is coming, what they want and which objections came up, so buyers never repeat themselves.
5. **Call no-shows the same day** with a friendly reschedule offer. Many are simple schedule clashes.

## Part 3: After the visit

The visit is not the finish line.

- Message the same day and thank them.
- Share what they asked about, such as a price sheet or payment plan.
- Agree the next step before they leave: a family visit, a call on a set day, a token discussion.
- Log what they liked, what worried them and who else decides.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Booking unqualified visits to hit a target
- Skipping confirmation and reminders
- Separate systems for telecallers and site team
- Ignoring no-shows

## A realistic example

A telecaller books a Saturday 11 am visit and sends nothing more. On Saturday morning the buyer is stuck with a family commitment, does not tell anyone and never shows. Now imagine the same booking with a WhatsApp confirmation, a pin, a reminder on Friday and a morning message with the site contact. The buyer replies, "Can we make it 4 pm?" and the visit is saved. The difference is one small, automatic nudge.

## Tips for the day itself

- Greet the buyer by name and ask what they most want to see.
- Show the unit and floor they care about before the general tour.
- Let the buyer talk. Their questions tell you what is stopping a decision.
- Offer refreshments and a comfortable place to discuss numbers.
- Never rush to the price sheet before they have seen what they are buying.

## Use data to find where visits drop

Break your visit numbers down by source and by salesperson. If one source brings many scheduled visits but few completed ones, the leads may be less serious, or the booking call may be promising too much too soon. If one salesperson has a much higher completion rate, ask what they do differently: they may confirm on WhatsApp, call the day before or set clearer expectations. Share those habits with the whole team and the whole funnel improves.

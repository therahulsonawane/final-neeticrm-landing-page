**The problem:** you need a CRM, but you have seen generic tools fail in real estate. Your leads come from portals and Meta ads, your buyers live on WhatsApp, your salespeople spend half the day on site, and the owner wants bookings by source, not just lead counts.

Most CRM purchases fail because the tool was not built for that. Run these nine checks before you compare prices.

## The nine checks

### 1. Does it capture every lead source?

List yours: Meta, Google, 99acres, MagicBricks, Housing.com, website forms, referrals, walk-ins, event CSVs. Ask to see each flowing in. If you will still copy and paste from emails, you have bought a nicer spreadsheet.

### 2. Does it assign and notify instantly?

Value starts when a lead arrives. Check automatic assignment by round-robin or project, plus phone notifications. See [why response speed matters](/blog/speed-to-lead-real-estate/).

### 3. Is the mobile app genuinely good?

Can a salesperson see priority leads, call, log an outcome in two taps and set a follow-up, even on weak network? A clumsy app means incomplete data and useless reports.

### 4. How does it handle WhatsApp?

Look for brochures, templates, automatic follow-ups and the whole conversation attached to the lead. Ask whether it uses the official WhatsApp Business platform. Our page on [WhatsApp CRM](/whatsapp-crm-for-real-estate/) explains what to expect.

### 5. Does it manage follow-ups and site visits?

A contact list is not a CRM. You need reminders that escalate when overdue, visit scheduling, and a way to mark who turned up.

### 6. Does it show cost per site visit and booking?

Cost per lead shows the cheapest source; cost per booking shows the profitable one, and they differ. Read [cost per lead vs cost per booking](/blog/cost-per-booking-real-estate/).

{{fig:project|A funnel report splitting cost per lead, site visit and booking by source.}}

### 7. Does it help you coach the team?

Call recording with AI analysis, objection help and agent scorecards turn a CRM into a coaching tool. See [AI call analysis](/ai-call-analysis-for-real-estate-sales/).

### 8. Who sets it up?

A vendor that hands you an empty account gives you a project to run. Look for assisted setup and a trial that starts only once the essentials are live, so you judge it on real leads.

### 9. Is pricing clear and flexible?

Check per-user pricing, extra charges, AI usage limits and commitment discounts, and make sure you can start small and move up a plan without migrating data. See our [pricing page](/real-estate-crm-pricing/).

## Score each CRM

Give every check 0 (missing), 1 (partial) or 2 (strong). Total the scores, but weigh what matters most to you: a small channel partner may prioritise mobile and WhatsApp, a developer may care most about project-level reporting.

## Red flags

- The demo uses made-up data and never shows your sources.
- Only a desktop view is shown.
- Reports stop at lead counts.
- Nobody can explain how follow-ups are enforced.

## How to run the trial properly

Many teams test a CRM with demo data and are surprised later. Instead, import a real week of leads, connect two of your actual sources and give three salespeople the mobile app. Set simple success measures before you start: average time to first call, share of follow-ups completed on time and whether salespeople log outcomes without being chased.

At the end, ask the team three questions. Was it faster than what we do today? Did it remind me of things I would have forgotten? Would I miss it if it were taken away? If the answers are yes, you have found a tool that fits how you sell.

## Common buying mistakes

- Choosing on price per user alone, then paying for missing features elsewhere
- Letting only the owner try the demo, not the people who will use it daily
- Skipping the question of who configures the system

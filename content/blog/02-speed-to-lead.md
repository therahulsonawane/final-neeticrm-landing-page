**The problem:** a buyer enquires on Sunday evening, and your team calls on Monday afternoon. By then they have spoken to two other developers and half forgotten you. You are no longer following up an enquiry. You are cold calling.

Speed matters in real estate because buyers enquire on several portals and ads at once. The team that has the first real conversation frames the comparison and often earns the site visit.

## What is a good response time?

There is no magic number for every business, and be wary of anyone quoting a statistic without a source. The principle is simple: the sooner you respond, the more likely the buyer still remembers enquiring.

To set your own target:

1. Measure your current average over the last 100 leads.
2. Aim slightly ahead of today. If you average three hours, aim for thirty minutes, then fifteen.
3. Be strict in working hours and rely on automation after hours.
4. Track it weekly. A target nobody checks is a wish.

## Why teams miss the window

It is almost never laziness. The process makes speed hard:

- Nobody is told when a lead arrives.
- Leads are unassigned, so everyone assumes someone else will call.
- The owner is at a site visit and cannot answer.
- Leads arrive in the evening and at weekends.
- Calls are not logged, so managers cannot see attempts.

Telling people to try harder fixes none of these. Changing the system does.

## The solution: build a response system

### Capture every source in one place

Connect Meta, Google, portals and website forms to one pipeline. Any lead sitting in someone's inbox will always be slow.

### Assign and notify instantly

Use automatic assignment by round-robin or by project, with push notifications on the mobile app. Salespeople are rarely at a desk.

### Message while you call

An automatic WhatsApp message with the project name, brochure and visit link confirms the enquiry and gives the buyer something to read if they miss your call. See [WhatsApp CRM for real estate](/whatsapp-crm-for-real-estate/).

### Put a timer on the first call

A visible first-call timer on every new lead creates healthy pressure. When it expires, the manager sees a flag and can reassign.

### Plan for after hours

You do not need calls at 11 pm. You need an immediate WhatsApp message, a note that the lead came after hours, and a first-thing-in-the-morning call list sorted by arrival time.

### Retry quietly

The first attempt often goes unanswered. Plan a second call a few hours later, a WhatsApp the same day, and another try next morning. Many real buyers answer on the third attempt.

## What to say when they answer

Speed earns the conversation; the first thirty seconds keep it. Introduce yourself and the project in one sentence, confirm it is a good time, ask one open question about what they want, and end with a clear next step such as a site visit. Do not read out every feature.

## Measure it weekly

Report average time to first attempt, the share of leads contacted within target, and leads still untouched after 24 hours, by source and by salesperson. Our post on [stopping lead leakage](/blog/real-estate-lead-leakage/) shows where else leads slip away.

## A realistic example

A salesperson is on a site visit when a Meta lead arrives at 4:10 pm. Without a system, it waits until they are back at the desk. With one, the lead is assigned instantly, the phone buzzes, the buyer receives a WhatsApp message with the brochure, and if nobody calls within the target window the manager gets a flag and reassigns it. The buyer experiences a prompt, professional reply either way.

## Mistakes that slow teams down

- **Setting a target but not measuring it.** Without a report, the number drifts back to hours.
- **Forcing night calls.** It burns out the team and annoys buyers. Use automation after hours.
- **Leaving leads unassigned.** Shared pools create the bystander effect.
- **Skipping the context.** A fast call that knows nothing about the enquiry wastes the advantage, so show the project, source and history on the lead.

## Review exceptions daily

Spend five minutes each morning on leads that breached the target. Ask why: was the owner unavailable, was the lead assigned late, or was the number wrong? Patterns appear quickly.

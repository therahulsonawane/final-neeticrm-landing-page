**The problem:** the call is going well, then the buyer says "the price is too high" or "I will think about it", and your salesperson freezes, argues or gives up. Enquiries die at this exact moment.

An objection is not a rejection. It is the buyer telling you what stands between them and a decision. The fix is a method, plus practice on the most common ones. One rule first: **never promise returns or appreciation you cannot back up.**

## The method: LAER

1. **Listen** without interrupting.
2. **Acknowledge** so the buyer feels heard.
3. **Explore** with a question to find the real reason.
4. **Respond** with relevant facts and a next step.

## Ten objections and what to try

These are examples to adapt in your own words, not scripts to recite.

**1. "The price is too high."** Often they have seen a cheaper option or do not see the value yet. Ask what they are comparing with, then show what the price includes, such as parking, amenities and possession date.

**2. "I want to think about it."** There is usually an unspoken concern. Ask what the main thing is, and offer to share details now or arrange a family visit.

**3. "The location is too far."** They worry about commute, schools or resale. Share real travel times and planned infrastructure so they can judge with facts.

**4. "I will wait for prices to fall."** Do not predict the market. Offer current pricing, the payment plan and any valid offers so they decide with clear information.

**5. "I am looking at another project."** Good, they should compare. Ask what they like there, then compare possession dates, approvals and amenities honestly.

**6. "I am not sure about the builder."** This is trust. Share RERA registration, past projects and delivery record, and invite them to visit completed projects.

**7. "The payment plan does not suit me."** Ask about loan plans and when larger payments are comfortable, then look for a plan or unit that fits.

**8. "I need to ask my spouse or parents."** A real second decision-maker. Offer a WhatsApp summary for them, or a visit when everyone can come.

**9. "I do not have time to visit."** Offer weekend or evening slots, or a short video walkthrough first.

**10. "Just send details on WhatsApp."** Ask what matters most, send exactly that, and say you will check in tomorrow.

## The solution: practise and learn from data

**Role-play weekly.** Practise these ten in short sessions, swap roles and keep a shared document of the best answers with your project-specific facts.

**Log the main objection** on every lost lead. If half say the location is too far, that is a targeting issue, not a skills issue.

**Coach from real calls.** Managers cannot hear every call, so teams use [AI call analysis](/ai-call-analysis-for-real-estate-sales/) to score conversations and flag missed objections. See [coaching with AI call analysis](/blog/ai-call-analysis-sales-coaching/).

## What good sounds like

- More questions than statements at first
- The buyer's own words repeated back
- Facts, never hype
- No arguing
- A clear next step at the end

## A realistic example

A buyer says, "It is too expensive." A weak reply defends the price. A stronger one asks, "Compared with what?" It turns out they saw a unit in another project that was cheaper but had no parking and a later possession date. Now you can compare like with like, and the conversation moves from price to value. The objection did not change; the question did.

## What to avoid

- Arguing or correcting the buyer sharply
- Reading a script word for word
- Offering discounts you are not authorised to give
- Making claims you cannot support, especially about returns
- Ending the call without a next step

Objection handling is a skill that improves with repetition, so make practice a weekly habit rather than a one-off workshop.

## Build your own objection library

Every project has objections that only your team hears, such as a particular road, a nearby construction site or a possession date. Keep a shared document with the objection, what the buyer usually means, the best factual answer and any supporting proof such as a map or approval. Update it monthly. New joiners then learn in days what used to take months.

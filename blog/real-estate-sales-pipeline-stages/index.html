Messy Real Estate Sales Pipeline? A Simple 7-Stage Fix

A good pipeline tells every salesperson what to do next and tells the owner where deals are stuck. Here is a clear set of stages.

By Neeti CRM Team27 Nov 20264 min read

The problem: ask ten teams how their pipeline works and you get ten answers. Some have twenty stages, some have two, and "interested" means different things to different salespeople. The owner cannot tell where deals are stuck.

A pipeline is a shared language. When everyone agrees what "site visit scheduled" means, a manager can read the board and know where to help. Here is a template that suits developers, mandate firms and channel partners.

The seven stages

1. New lead. An enquiry arrived and nobody has spoken to the buyer. *Exit:* a real conversation, or a set number of attempts over several days. Watch time to first call, as in calling leads faster.

2. Contacted. You have spoken, but needs are unconfirmed. *Exit:* you know budget, unit type, timeline and decision-makers.

3. Qualified. A real need fits your project with a plausible budget and timeline. *Exit:* a visit is proposed with a date discussed.

4. Site visit scheduled. A date and time are confirmed. *Exit:* the visit happens or is rescheduled. Watch scheduled versus completed; see getting more site visits.

5. Site visit completed. The buyer has seen the project. *Exit:* you know their favourite unit, their concerns and the next step.

6. Negotiation. The buyer is seriously considering a unit and you are discussing price, payment plan or timing. *Exit:* token or booking received, or a clear no.

7. Booked. A booking is confirmed and goes to documentation. Track cost per booking by source, as in cost per lead vs cost per booking.

Closed-lost, with a reason. Every lead that stops should carry a reason: budget, location, bought elsewhere, not responding or wrong enquiry. Reasons tell you what to fix in marketing, pricing or sales.

Keep stages and outcomes apart

"Did not answer" is not a stage; it is the result of a call. If every outcome becomes a stage, the pipeline turns into a long, confusing list. Record outcomes after each call, such as interested, follow up, call back later, not interested or wrong number, and move the stage only when something meaningful happens. The Neeti CRM mobile app captures outcomes in two taps.

The solution: add rules, not just columns

  • Entry rules: what must be true to enter a stage
  • Exit rules: what must happen before moving on
  • Time limits: how long a lead may sit before someone looks
  • Required notes: what must be recorded

A lead sitting in "Contacted" for two weeks is a warning sign, and a good CRM flags it automatically.

Roll it out

  1. Agree the stages with your sales leads in one meeting.
  2. Write one sentence per stage for meaning and exit rule.
  3. Configure the CRM and import existing leads into the right stages.
  4. Train the team and use it daily.
  5. Review conversion and stage times weekly.

A realistic example

A mandate firm sells three developers' projects. Its old board had stages like "hot", "warm" and "follow up", and every salesperson used them differently. After moving to the seven stages above, the owner discovered that most leads stalled between "Qualified" and "Site visit scheduled". The fix was not more calls, but a rule that every qualified buyer gets two visit options on the same day. The pipeline made the problem visible.

Common pipeline mistakes

  • Too many stages, so nobody keeps them accurate
  • Stages named after feelings, such as "hot", rather than events
  • No exit rules, so leads move on gut feel
  • No closed-lost reasons, so the same losses repeat

Frequently asked questions

How many stages should a pipeline have?

Six to eight works for most teams. Fewer hides problems; more becomes hard to keep accurate.

Stage or status?

A stage shows where the buyer is in the journey; a status describes the latest interaction, such as did not answer. Keep them separate.

Do mandate firms and CPs need different pipelines?

The core is the same. Mandate firms add project-level views; CPs may add a stage for sharing a buyer with a developer's team.
